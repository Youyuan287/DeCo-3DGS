                                                                                    Abstract

                                                  Open-vocabulary 3D Gaussian segmentation typically binds semantic features
                                                  to the per-Gaussian state via per-scene gradient training (taking 1-4 h) or the
                                                  more recent closed-form distillation. Both commit each primitive to a single
                                                  feature; therefore, boundary Gaussians spanning multiple 2D instances are forced
                                                  into a hard decision that degrades retrieval accuracy and yields unstable mask
                                                  boundaries at render time. We present DIGS, a training-free framework with two
                                                  stages, class-agnostic mapping and multi-modal encoding, that decouples instance
                                                  discovery from linguistic naming. At the Gaussian level, our distribution-informed
                                                  representation maintains an explicit top-M label distribution per primitive, updated
                                                  by a tempered Dirichlet rule; preservation of multi-peaked label distributions
                                                  at boundary primitives allows a late-argmax renderer to recover clean instance
                                                  boundaries. A bidirectional state machine aggregates these distributions into a
                                                  persistent class-agnostic instance ontology. The instance-level encoding stage then
                                                  fuses the CLIP visual embedding of each instance's isolated 3D footprint with the
                                                  CLIP-text embedding of an LLM-generated attribute description, disambiguating
                                                  visually similar instances. On a single A100, DIGS completes mapping and
                                                  instance encoding on LERF-OVS in 1.0-3.2 min per scene, depending on whether
                                                  LLM fusion is enabled, and serves native 3D queries in 5.4 ms. It reaches 71.95%
                                                  mean mIoU on LERF-OVS, outperforming both the training-free SFS and training-
                                                  based LaGa baselines. DIGS also achieves the highest mean mIoU on LERF-Mask
                                                  and 3D-OVS and surpasses Gaga and ObjectGS on Replica under their respective
                                                  evaluation protocols. Code is available at https://github.com/ZephyrZos/
                                                  DIGS.

                                        1 Introduction

                                        Semantic understanding of a 3D environment from sequential observations is essential for embodied
                                        agents [1], virtual reality [2], and language-driven scene editing [3]. Open-vocabulary segmentation
                                        on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [4] requires a per-Gaussian semantic representation that supports
                                        text queries. Prior methods bind a single semantic feature (a continuous CLIP embedding or a
                                        clustered instance ID) to each Gaussian via per-scene gradient training [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. This
                                        design couples two failures. First, boundary Gaussians spanning multiple 2D instances are forced
                                        to one feature, blurring rendered edges. Second, the per-scene optimisation (taking 1-4 h) bakes
                                        the language signal into the per-Gaussian state, locking each reconstructed asset to one language
                                        model. Recent work eases one symptom at a time. The closed-form Splat Feature Solver [11] reduces
                                        training to minutes but inherits the boundary blur. ObjectGS [12] sharpens boundaries by splitting
                                        cross-instance Gaussians under joint photometric and cross-entropy optimisation, which can hurt
                                        RGB fidelity when the 2D mask supervision is noisy. The Bayesian alternative [13] treats each
                                        query as a binary Beta-Bernoulli problem. No prior design, however, resolves the coupling between
                                        training cost and boundary fidelity within a single per-Gaussian representation.

The two failures are not independent symptoms but consequences of what each Gaussian stores as
the semantic state, as Figure 1a illustrates. A single one-hot label (e.g., ObjectGS [12]) yields sharp
argmax-composited boundaries, but the discrete cross-entropy loss forces boundary Gaussians to split
during training, degrading the reconstructed RGB whenever the 2D mask supervision is noisy or view-
inconsistent. A continuous CLIP-style feature (e.g., LangSplat [5]) tolerates approximate geometry,
but linear blending of adjacent-instance embeddings at boundary pixels produces semantically
ambiguous mixtures that may match neither class at query time. Both choices commit each primitive
to a single semantic mode: the discrete one pays in geometry, the continuous one in semantics.
Neither breaks the boundary-cost coupling.

We present DIGS, a training-free framework whose distribution-informed per-Gaussian representation
stores an explicit top-M label distribution, updated by a tempered Dirichlet rule. Targeting the
boundary failure, the distribution is composited at the pixel level via late-argmax rendering; boundary
Gaussians contribute proportionally to adjacent instances and the final label is decided by the majority
of unambiguous neighbours, lifting LERF-Mask mean mIoU to 91.71 (+3.3 over the previous best
ObjectGS at 88.4) and adding +8.84 mean mIoU in the LERF-OVS distribution-preserving ablation
(§4.3). Targeting the cost-coupling failure, the tempered Dirichlet update is closed-form with a
provably bounded per-frame injection (Proposition 1) and executes as a single parallel pass per frame,
completing class-agnostic mapping in 0.91 min on a single A100--versus 1-4 h of per-scene gradient
training for distillation baselines, a 60-250× reduction, as Figure 1b visualises.

On LERF-OVS, these choices place DIGS at 71.95 mean mIoU, surpassing the strongest training-free
baseline SFS and the strongest training-based baseline LaGa, defining a new accuracy/wall-time
frontier. The advantage sharpens on adversarial prompts: as Figure 1c shows on yellow desk in
Waldo_kitchen, where LaGa, LangSplatV2, and SFS all leak the mask into surrounding fixtures,
DIGS returns a clean boundary that covers more of the object than even the human-annotated
ground truth. These outcomes follow from three contributions: a distribution-informed per-Gaussian
representation (top-M label distribution) that resolves the boundary failure via late-argmax rendering;
a closed-form tempered Dirichlet voting rule with a provably bounded per-frame injection that yields
a class-agnostic instance ontology in a single parallel pass without per-scene gradient training; and a
post-hoc multi-modal encoding that fuses each instance's CLIP visual embedding with the CLIP-text
embedding of an LLM-generated attribute description, keeping the mapping output reusable across
language backbones.

2 Related Work

3D scene semantic segmentation. Since 3D Gaussian Splatting [4] became a mainstream scene
representation, a growing body of work has addressed how to assign semantic or instance information

                                                                                      2
to its primitives. The dominant recipe uses a 2D class-agnostic mask generator, typically SAM [14],
and lifts its outputs onto the Gaussians through per-scene optimisation [9, 15, 16, 17, 18]. This
inherits the 2D-to-3D lifting tradition from neural-field predecessors: Semantic-NeRF [19] and
Panoptic Lifting [20] embed per-frame labels into implicit fields, while Contrastive Lift [21] and
SA3D [22] lift instance or SAM masks; all are bottlenecked by the slow volumetric rendering of their
underlying representation. Earlier point-cloud methods [23, 24] train from scratch on annotated 3D
data, and open-vocabulary point-cloud systems [25, 26, 27] extend this to CLIP-driven text queries. A
complementary line of work casts the lifting problem as a one-shot assignment: FlashSplat [28] solves
it using linear programming with an optimal closed-form solution but operates on a single target mask
per query; B3-Seg [13] formulates segmentation as sequential per-query Beta-Bernoulli Bayesian
updates, without a persistent multi-instance ontology; Splat Feature Solver [11] casts feature lifting as
a closed-form sparse linear inverse problem, achieving CLIP feature fields at minute-scale per-scene
but committing each Gaussian to a single continuous feature without per-primitive label uncertainty;
and Occam's LGS [29] formulates language-feature assignment as a MAP-based weighted multi-view
aggregation, training-free but again binding each Gaussian to a single CLIP feature. Most closely
related to our two-stage design, DCSEG [30] and ExtrinSplat [31] also decouple class-agnostic 3D
grouping from downstream language assignment via a 2D foundation model, but require per-scene
optimisation. DIGS departs from these paradigms by performing multi-class instance lifting with an
explicit per-Gaussian top-M distribution in a single streaming pass, without gradient computation.

Per-Gaussian semantic representation. What each Gaussian stores as semantic state is a decisive
design axis. A first family distils a continuous vision-language feature into every Gaussian, extending
the feature-field distillation idea of DFF [32], N3F [33], and LERF [34] to Gaussian primitives:
LangSplat [5], LangSplatV2 [6], LEGaussians [7], and 3D-VLGS [35] all bind each Gaussian to
a CLIP embedding through tens of thousands of training iterations, producing a feature field tied
to the training-time language model. A second family supervises a per-Gaussian identity feature
through dense 2D masks in three forms: InfoNCE-style contrastive losses (OpenGaussian [8],
Unified-Lift [17], and OpenSplat3D [16]), cross-entropy classification over 2D mask IDs (Gaussian
Grouping [9] and ObjectGS [12]) and 3D-aware memory-bank grouping (Gaga [15]). All of them
commit each Gaussian to a single identity mode, enforced across frames by the training loss. A third
option uses a binary Bayesian posterior [13], but only for one target at a time; this is itself the binary
special case of a longer lineage of per-primitive Bayesian semantic aggregation on surfels [36] and
voxels [37, 38], whose multi-class Dirichlet-Categorical form we adopt as the starting point of our
voting rule. DIGS introduces a fourth option that none of the above provides: an explicit multi-peaked
discrete distribution over instance identities, maintained per Gaussian, updated by a closed-form
tempered Bayesian rule, and persistent across an arbitrary number of subsequent text queries.

Open-vocabulary querying and foundation models. Recent 3DGS open-vocabulary systems rely
on vision-language foundation models such as CLIP [39] and open-set detectors [40] to connect text
prompts with image content; methods that bind these models into the 3D representation at training
time inherit a tight coupling, since the choice of language model becomes part of the scene asset
itself. DIGS instead decouples lifting from language entirely: the mapping stage is class-agnostic,
and foundation models enter only at the downstream per-instance encoding step, so the same map
serves any text-driven query interface without re-running the lifting.

3 Method

3.1 Overview

DIGS operates in two decoupled stages on a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (Figure 2). Stage 1 (class-
agnostic mapping, §3.2-§3.5) builds a language-free instance ontology by distribution-informed
per-Gaussian voting (top-M state under a tempered Dirichlet rule). Stage 2 (multi-modal instance
encoding, §3.6) attaches a fused CLIP-visual / LLM-text embedding to each stable instance.

3.5 Distribution-Preserving Voting Pipeline

The voting pipeline drives the state machine (§3.4) forward under streaming observations, preserving
the top-M distribution end-to-end.

Predict step. Before observing the current frame, the state machine renders a predicted label
map L^(t) (Eq. (6)) with predicted regions {Rj}. SAM masks {i} are associated with global
instance IDs by Hungarian bipartite matching on an IoU cost; an IoU threshold iou decides new-ID
allocation versus match, and a greedy fallback permits many-to-one matching under occlusion-
induced fragmentation. The result is the global label map L(t)  ZH×W consumed by the update
step.

retrieval without re-running the voting pipeline.

open-vocabulary retrieval, requiring that the correct instance be both localised and fully segmented,

5 Conclusion

In this paper, we present DIGS, a training-free framework for open-vocabulary 3D Gaussian seg-
mentation. Existing methods bind a single semantic feature to each Gaussian via per-scene gradient
training, blurring boundary Gaussians and locking each scene to one language model. We addressed
this with two designs. First, a distribution-informed per-Gaussian representation: each primitive
stores a top-M label distribution updated under a tempered Dirichlet rule with a provably bounded
per-frame injection, allowing a late-argmax renderer to recover clean instance boundaries. Second, a
streaming voting pipeline that updates a bidirectional state machine into a class-agnostic instance
ontology in one closed-form Dirichlet pass per frame, removing per-scene gradient training. Open-
vocabulary querying is a lightweight post-hoc step fusing each instance's CLIP visual embedding
with an LLM-generated attribute description. On LERF-OVS, DIGS maps a scene in 1-3 minutes on
a single A100 and serves native 3D queries at 5.4 ms latency. It achieves state-of-the-art results on
LERF-OVS, LERF-Mask, 3D-OVS, and Replica, while its CLIP-only configuration maps LERF-OVS
scenes 225× faster than the strongest training-based baseline.

Limitations and perspectives. DIGS depends on its foundation-model stack: SAM's mask granu-
larity caps the finest discoverable instance, while GroundingDINO, CLIP, and LLM jointly bound
linguistic discrimination. Fusing multi-granularity SAM masks into a hierarchical ontology under
the same voting machinery is a natural next step. A second limitation is that the pre-reconstructed
Gaussian geometry is fixed throughout the semantic pipeline; jointly refining geometry and instance
evidence may further improve boundary fidelity on transparent or thin structures.

E.3 Failure Cases

Despite leading mean mIoU on all three benchmarks, DIGS exhibits two residual failure modes that
trace to upstream rendering and segmentation limits; representative examples are in Figures 8 and 9.

F1. Transparency couples voting weight to background. Our per-Gaussian voting weight Wi(t)
is the alpha-compositing weight from rendering, so transparent and translucent objects (glass / plastic
surfaces) propagate non-trivial weight through the foreground surface to background Gaussians at the
same pixel. The closed-loop association then assigns the foreground SAM mask partly to background
Gaussians, "smearing" the instance footprint past the transparent surface and inflating its 3D extent.
This is visible on the transparent box in Figurines and the transparent cup in Ramen.

F2. SAM-granularity caps part-level prompts. SAM's automatic-mask generator commits to
a single granularity per scene; when a prompt names a sub-region of a larger SAM mask, our
reconstructed semantic map has no slot for the part and retrieval returns the whole parent instance.
Affected queries on LERF-OVS include waldo (a printed character on the cabinet), pirate hat (part
of a figurine), and hooves together with dall-e brand (a body region and a printed logo on the same
figurine); in each case the relevant SAM mask covers the whole parent, capping the achievable IoU
on the part-level query. This reflects the SAM-granularity ceiling discussed in §5 (Limitations and
outlook).
